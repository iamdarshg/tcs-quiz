Practice Questions

Q1: Which goal belongs to cybersecurity?
  1. Protecting systems, networks, and data from attack ←
  2. Increasing every network's latency
  3. Publishing all personal records
  4. Removing authentication controls
Cybersecurity protects confidentiality, integrity, and availability through people, processes, and technical safeguards.
Q2: An email imitates a bank and links to a fake login page. What attack is this?
  1. Distributed denial of service
  2. Phishing ←
  3. Credential stuffing
  4. DNS cache poisoning
Phishing uses deceptive messages and websites to make a victim reveal credentials or run malicious content.
Q3: Which behaviour defines ransomware most directly?
  1. It blocks or encrypts data and demands payment ←
  2. It secretly mines cryptocurrency without an extortion demand
  3. It records keystrokes to steal credentials
  4. It recruits a device into a traffic-flooding botnet
Ransomware is extortion malware: access is denied and restoration is offered in exchange for money, often without any guarantee.
Q4: Readable plaintext becomes what after encryption?
  1. Ciphertext ←
  2. Hash digest
  3. Encoded text
  4. Compressed archive
Encryption transforms plaintext into ciphertext that should be intelligible only with the authorised cryptographic key.
Q5: Which question is principally about data privacy rather than service availability?
  1. Why was my location collected and shared? ←
  2. Can the service recover from a traffic flood?
  3. Were account records altered without permission?
  4. Can an authorised user retrieve the backup?
Privacy concerns the legitimate collection, purpose, retention, and sharing of personal information such as location data.
Q6: Thousands of compromised devices flood one site until genuine users cannot connect. Identify the attack.
  1. Phishing
  2. Ransomware
  3. Distributed denial of service ←
  4. Encryption at rest
A DDoS attack coordinates traffic from many systems to exhaust a target and damage service availability.
Q7: How can phishing and ransomware be related without being synonyms?
  1. Phishing may deliver ransomware or stolen access ←
  2. Both always mean network flooding
  3. Ransomware is a privacy law
  4. Phishing is protective encryption
Phishing is the deception or delivery route; ransomware is the malware-driven extortion that may follow initial access.
Q8: Which contrast is accurate?
  1. Legitimate encryption protects authorised access; ransomware withholds access ←
  2. All encryption is ransomware
  3. Ciphertext is always public plaintext
  4. Privacy makes cryptography unnecessary
The cryptographic operation can be similar, but purpose and key control distinguish a safeguard from a ransomware attack.
Q9: A company encrypts records but collects them without a lawful purpose. What remains unresolved?
  1. Data privacy governance ←
  2. Authentication strength
  3. Encryption-key rotation
  4. Disaster-recovery testing
Encryption supports confidentiality, but privacy also demands lawful purpose, minimisation, retention limits, and accountable use.
Q10: Which security property is the main target of a DDoS flood?
  1. Availability ←
  2. Confidentiality
  3. Integrity
  4. Authenticity
By preventing legitimate users from reaching a service, denial-of-service attacks principally undermine availability.
