Practice Questions

Q1: Which sequence best represents the scientific method?
  1. Observation, hypothesis, test, revision ←
  2. Hypothesis, conclusion, observation, test
  3. Test, revision, observation, hypothesis
  4. Conclusion, hypothesis, revision, observation
Scientific inquiry moves from observations to testable explanations, gathers evidence, and revises conclusions when results demand it.
Q2: What does vaccination prepare the body to do?
  1. Recognise a pathogen before serious disease ←
  2. Kill every bacterium directly on injection
  3. Supply permanent ready-made antibodies
  4. Replace inherited DNA with pathogen DNA
Vaccines expose the immune system to an antigen or its instructions so memory responses can act rapidly on later exposure.
Q3: Which molecule carries hereditary information in most living organisms?
  1. DNA ←
  2. RNA
  3. Protein
  4. Lipid
DNA encodes genetic information in nucleotide sequences and passes that information through cell division and reproduction.
Q4: Which illness would not be cured merely by taking an antibiotic?
  1. Streptococcal throat infection
  2. Bacterial urinary infection
  3. Influenza caused by a virus ←
  4. Bacterial pneumonia
Antibiotics target bacterial structures or processes; influenza is viral and therefore needs prevention or antiviral management rather than antibacterial drugs.
Q5: A finite sequence of unambiguous problem-solving steps is called what?
  1. Heuristic
  2. Algorithm ←
  3. Hypothesis
  4. Mathematical model
An algorithm specifies a bounded, defined procedure whose steps transform inputs or state toward a result.
Q6: On the conventional 0-to-1 scale, what probability represents an impossible event?
  1. 0 ←
  2. 0.25
  3. 0.5
  4. 1
Probability zero denotes impossibility in the elementary scale, while one denotes certainty and values between represent uncertainty.
Q7: Why are controlled vaccine trials part of the scientific method?
  1. They test safety and effectiveness claims against evidence ←
  2. They prove a hypothesis before participants are observed
  3. They remove the need for a comparison or control group
  4. They guarantee that later evidence cannot revise a conclusion
Controlled comparison lets researchers estimate effects and uncertainty instead of accepting a medical claim on authority alone.
Q8: Which contrast is correct?
  1. Vaccines prime immune memory; antibiotics act against bacteria ←
  2. Vaccines directly kill all bacteria; antibiotics train immune memory
  3. Antibiotics prevent viral infection; vaccines treat every active infection
  4. Vaccines and antibiotics are interchangeable pathogen treatments
Vaccination is preventive immune training, while antibiotics are treatments directed at bacterial infections.
Q9: How can DNA be linked to antibiotic resistance?
  1. Resistance genes or mutations can be carried in bacterial DNA ←
  2. Resistance occurs only when human DNA changes after treatment
  3. Antibiotics prevent bacteria from inheriting any DNA
  4. Bacterial resistance is independent of mutation and gene transfer
Bacteria can inherit or exchange genetic changes that reduce a drug's effect, allowing resistant populations to survive.
Q10: A navigation program chooses routes using estimated traffic likelihoods. Which two ideas are combined?
  1. Algorithm and probability ←
  2. Database indexing and encryption
  3. Computer graphics and compression
  4. Network routing and authentication
The route procedure is an algorithm, and its uncertain traffic estimates are expressed with probabilistic information.
